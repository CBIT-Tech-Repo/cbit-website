From a first cohort to a sister centre

Relationships grow one rung at a time. With Chiang Mai University and Thailand’s Ministry of Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation (MHESI), CBIT took about two years to climb from a proposed memorandum of understanding to a five-year national memorandum and a sister centre.

In cooperation with Chiang Mai University’s Science and Technology Park (STeP); the national AI skills programme was funded by MHESI.

  1. Rung 1

    Meet

    A keynote, a masterclass, a visit or a delegation.

    • 2024 A proposed memorandum of understanding (May), a memorandum between NTU and Chiang Mai University (October), and a staff and venture exchange
    • November 2025 MHESI’s visit to NTU

  2. Rung 2

    Try one thing

    One cohort, one course or one venture.

    • November 2024 The first founder cohort in the UK
    • November 2025 A second cohort

  3. Rung 3

    Fund it together

    A joint grant.

    • 2025 A joint British Council grant, under the International Science Partnerships Fund

  4. Rung 4

    Transfer the method

    Train the partner’s own staff.

    • December 2025 Train-the-trainer in Thailand

  5. Rung 5

    Scale

    A national contract, or several programmes.

    • March to August 2026 MHESI’s national AI skills programme

  6. Rung 6

    Anchor

    A five-year memorandum of understanding and a sister centre.

    • April 2026 A five-year memorandum with MHESI, and a sister centre at STeP

Founders and SMEs

Start or grow a venture. Everything for founders and SMEs

  • CBIT Venture Builder

    A UK air-purifier maker grew with the Venture Builder, then joined a funded project in Thailand

    In their words: In a testimonial, Healthy Air says the support “enabled us to enter four new countries”.

    Who: Healthy Air Technology, a UK maker of air-purification products and a member of the CBIT Venture Builder cohort.

    Read the full story: A UK air-purifier maker grew with the Venture Builder, then joined a funded project in Thailand

    The challenge
    The company was developing smart air purifiers and wanted to grow beyond the UK.
    What CBIT did
    CBIT supported it through the CBIT Venture Builder, CBIT’s programme of one-to-one support for founders and SMEs. In 2024 the company joined CBIT’s British Council bid with Chiang Mai University as an associated partner and “venture in residence”.
    What happened
    The bid won funding in 2025. Healthy Air is part of the project as a UK venture committed to exploring the Thai market with CBIT.
    Where it can lead
    Ventures can join CBIT’s funded projects as partners, or seek innovation funding with CBIT.

    More about the CBIT Venture Builder

  • Global VB Partnership

    A French energy-optimisation firm grew in the UK market with Venture Builder support

    In their words: “CBIT Venture Builder has accelerated OAK Network’s expansion in the UK market.” Oak Network, in a testimonial.

    Who: Oak Network, an energy-optimisation company based in France.

    Read the full story: A French energy-optimisation firm grew in the UK market with Venture Builder support

    The challenge
    Oak Network wanted to expand into the UK market.
    What CBIT did
    CBIT supported the company through the Venture Builder in 2024–25 as it expanded into the UK. Ventures from outside the UK now reach the same support through the Global VB Partnership, directly or through a partner organisation.
    What happened
    Oak Network’s UK expansion went ahead with the Venture Builder’s support, and the company credits CBIT with speeding it up.
    Where it can lead
    A venture can go on to a funded project with CBIT, for example a Knowledge Transfer Partnership. For partner organisations abroad, the same route lands their ventures in the UK.

    More about the Global VB Partnership

  • AI-Empowered Going Global

    Chinese technology CEOs came to Nottingham to work on taking their businesses global

    Who: Chief executives and founders of Chinese technology companies, in an executive cohort from Fudan University School of Management.

    Read the full story: Chinese technology CEOs came to Nottingham to work on taking their businesses global

    The challenge
    The CEOs needed to see where AI creates value in their businesses, and how to grow beyond China.
    What CBIT did
    CBIT designed a bespoke three-day strategy lab for the cohort: where AI was failing to create value, a charter for AI agents with a rule for when to stop, a 90-day experiment plan, a company visit and pitches.
    What happened
    The lab ran in Nottingham in July 2026, the month CBIT and the school signed a five-year sister-centre partnership.
    Where it can lead
    Further executive cohorts can follow.

    More about AI-Empowered Going Global

Win interdisciplinary research, and turn it into ventures and impact. Everything for research and university leaders

  • Interdisciplinary research bids

    Many disciplines, one method: UKRI’s panel graded the BeefTwin bid A*

    In the funder’s words: UKRI’s panel called it an “outstanding proposal which has very strong potential to result in key and transformative change”.

    Who: BeefTwin, a UKRI-funded consortium of universities, farms and industry, led by CBIT at Nottingham Trent University.

    Read the full story: Many disciplines, one method: UKRI’s panel graded the BeefTwin bid A*

    The challenge
    Feed, farming and food pull on income, welfare and emissions at once, a triple-bottom-line problem no single discipline can balance.
    What CBIT did
    CBIT built the bid on its Sparring Partnership method, where disciplines challenge each other’s ideas, and on lean start-up sprints of build, measure and learn.
    What happened
    UKRI funded the bid. Professor Xiao Ma, CBIT’s Director, led it.
    Track record
    BeefTwin £1.2m (Project Lead, Round 1) and EASE £1.2m (Project Co-Lead, Round 2) — the only UK researcher to have won awards in both rounds of UKRI's Cross-Research Council Responsive Mode. EASE is co-led with Dr Iryna Kuksa.
    Where it can lead
    Bring us into your next bid early.

    More about interdisciplinary research bids

  • Venture partner for your bid

    An art and climate-science consortium brought CBIT in as its venture engine, and won UKRI funding

    In the funder’s words: UKRI’s assessment panel valued the “strong interdisciplinary track record of the proposed team”.

    Who: EASE (Entrepreneurship, Art and Science for Environmental Sustainability), a consortium led by De Montfort University with Nottingham Trent University, the University of Birmingham and the University of East London.

    Read the full story: An art and climate-science consortium brought CBIT in as its venture engine, and won UKRI funding

    The challenge
    EASE set out to turn artists’ ideas on climate communication into tested prototypes that people could use.
    What CBIT did
    CBIT leads the “Build & Test” work: Artist Venturing Labs develop prototypes through creative sprints and lean start-up methods, and the project tests them for social, environmental and economic value. NTU adds Venture Builder support in kind.
    What happened
    UKRI’s panel graded the application Band A, and UKRI funded it in the scheme’s second round. The project is now live.
    Where it can lead
    Viable prototypes can move into the CBIT Venture Builder, and the partners can bid for funding together again.

    More about a venture partner for your bid
